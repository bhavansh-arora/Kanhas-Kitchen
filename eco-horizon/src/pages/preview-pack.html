Where quotes slip away

Three doors. One generic form.

We walked the quote path as a homeowner, a business owner and a farmer. Here's what we found.

02
01 · Same form for everyone

Homes, Business and Agri all end at one form

A homeowner, a 200 kWp factory and a dairy farm get the same fields and a "Select Solar Type" dropdown. Nothing captures bill size, roof, timeline or battery and EV interest, so every lead needs a qualifying call.

02 · Details before value

The form opens with "Your Name"

Visitors are asked for name, company and phone before they've seen what solar could save them. People still researching leave at this point, and research is where most solar buyers start.

03 · No number to react to

Grants are explained, not calculated

The grant tables are accurate, but visitors have to do the maths themselves. A number like "€2,400 back" makes people act. A paragraph about €700 per kWp mostly gets skimmed.

04 · Farm grant confusion

TAMS 3 vs SEAI is left to the farmer

Both schemes are described, but the farmer has to work out which pays more and that they can't claim both. That uncertainty stops a lot of agri enquiries.

05 · Upsell left on the table

Batteries and EV chargers read as spec sheets

Great products, but nothing shows a homeowner why they'd want one. The battery decision is where the bigger orders are won.

06 · Small copy slips

Homeowner pages say "your business"

The shared 3-step block on the residential page talks about "your business" and "your company", and has a small typo ("space space"). Minor, but careful buyers notice.
